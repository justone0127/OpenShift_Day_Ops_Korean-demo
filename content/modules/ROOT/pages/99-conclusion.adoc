[cols="1,2,2",options="header"]
|===
| Attack stage | What happened at RedPay | Security control we applied

| Stage 1
| Logged in to an operator account with a single leaked password
| *Module 1 · Red Hat build of Keycloak* — OTP-based multi-factor authentication (MFA) enforced for every user

| Stage 2
| Every service trusted the attacker just because the traffic came from an internal range
| *Module 2 · Zero Trust Workload Identity Manager* — Verification by cryptographic identity (mTLS), not network location

| Stage 3
| Data exfiltrated through an RCE-vulnerable library in an image tagged `latest`
| *Module 3 · Red Hat Advanced Cluster Security* — A detection-only policy switched to blocking at deploy time
|===

Each control is valuable on its own, but together they mean something more.
An attack proceeds one stage at a time, and **if it is stopped at any one stage, what follows never happens.**

What brought RedPay down was not a sophisticated attack. MFA, workload identity and image policies —
**all three were security it already had, left sitting under "we'll turn it on later".**

To learn more, see Red Hat's https://github.com/validatedpatterns/layered-zero-trust[Layered Zero Trust Validated Pattern].
